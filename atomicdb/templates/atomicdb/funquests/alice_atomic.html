Alice Atomic

Play the computer

It thinks for about a second a move, in your browser.

Play a friend

You get your own private link and one to send to your friend. No account needed.

Send this link to your opponent. The game starts when they open it. Keep the address of this page: it is your own way back into the game.

    Moves are written as the board letter and the move: Ae2e4 is the pawn on e2 of board A going to e4. Dots show where the selected piece can go; a faint ring on the other board shows where it will land.

    The rules

    • Two boards. Board A starts with the usual chess position; board B starts empty. White moves first.
    • Through the looking glass. A piece moves on the board it stands on, following the normal chess moves of that board; only pieces on that same board get in its way. A move that does not capture also needs the same square on the other board to be empty, and after the move the piece passes through to that square of the other board.
    • Captures explode. A capture happens on the board where the move is made and explodes there: the captured piece, the capturing piece and every piece except pawns on the eight squares around it are removed from that board. The capturing piece is destroyed, so nothing passes through on a capture, and the other board does not need to be empty there. The blast never reaches the other board. Kings cannot capture.
    • En passant. After a pawn's double step it stands on the other board. On the very next move, an enemy pawn on that same board, beside it, may take it en passant, moving to the square it passed over (that square must be empty on that board). The blast is centred on that square.
    • Promotion works as usual. A promotion without a capture passes through to the other board like any quiet move; a capturing promotion explodes.
    • Castling. King and rook must not have moved, the squares between them must be empty, and the king must not be in check, pass through or land on an attacked square, all on their board. The squares where the king and the rook land must also be empty on the other board, and both pass through to it.
    • Check. A king is in check when an enemy piece on the same board attacks it, except when the two kings stand next to each other on that board: then neither checks the other. You may not leave your king in check, and you may not make a capture that blows up your own king.
    • Winning. Blowing up the enemy king wins at once, even if your own king is left attacked. Checkmate also wins.
    • Draws. Stalemate, the same position three times with the same player to move, fifty moves by each side without a capture or a pawn move, or a draw agreed between the players.
