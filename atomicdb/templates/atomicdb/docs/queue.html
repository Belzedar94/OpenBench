The queue

One commons, and it is public at the queue page: one row per account, read from the same predicates the ordering uses, so the page and the queue cannot disagree without it being a bug in one place.

  • Every account advances by the nodes it already has queued, so the first request of a newcomer is served before the thousandth of a flooder. The unit is nodes and not rows, so one 2B request weighs as much as about sixteen 128M requests, and a big batch interleaves instead of walling the queue.
  • Running a worker gives exactly one advantage: while it runs, your own worker serves your own requests first. It is affinity and not priority, and it scales with the CPU you are plugging in right now.
  • The cap is per person: {{ queue_cap }} queued requests, pending rows only. Cancelled rows do not count, running rows do not count, and clearing your queue gives the allowance back immediately.
  • Backing a request charges it to the best placed of its requesters, which is what makes backing move things. Authorship never moves; only the charge does.

Asking for a position somebody else already asked for joins their request instead of queueing a duplicate, and every account that asked is notified when the analysis lands. Repeated requests buy genuinely deeper searches, up the ladder of 128M, 512M and 2B nodes. Signed in, you can also tick several moves in the move list of a position and request them all with one button, up to {{ multi_request_cap }} at a time. Each ticked move is one request, as if its own button had been clicked, with two differences. If you may pick a depth for that button, the bar offers a depth of its own, any rung from 128M to 2B whatever the depth of the position you are on, and a depth picked there is a target: a move with no search that deep gets exactly that depth, and a move already searched that deep, as its depth column shows, is skipped. And with no depth picked, a move already searched to the top of the ladder is left as it is; open it to queue the searches below it.

Once the 2B search of a position has run, the next request queues up to {{ subtree_cap }} searches of 128M on the lines below it instead, the best lines first: where the side the position favours is to move, its three best moves (eight when nothing ranks them yet); where the other side is to move, every reply. A position that already has its 128M search is walked through and its own replies are asked next. Asking again while those searches are still waiting queues nothing new. The last stop of the depth slider buys the same searches without waiting for the ladder.
